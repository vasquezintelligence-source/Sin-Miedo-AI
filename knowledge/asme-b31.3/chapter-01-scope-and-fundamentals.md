# ASME B31.3 — Chapter I: Scope, Governing Code, and Fundamentals

## Lesson 1A — Identify the governing code

The first responsibility of a QC Manager is determining whether B31.3 actually governs the work.

B31.3 addresses process piping used in facilities such as refineries and chemical plants. It covers design, materials, fabrication, assembly, erection, examination, inspection, and testing.

| Equipment or System | Potential Governing Code |
|---|---|
| Refinery process piping | ASME B31.3 |
| Power plant boiler external piping | ASME B31.1, with applicable boiler-code requirements |
| Pressure vessel shell or nozzle construction | ASME Section VIII |

The correct answer depends on the equipment boundaries, applicable jurisdiction, original construction code, and project specifications.

## Lesson 1B — Fluid service classification

B31.3 includes different fluid-service categories with different requirements.

| Classification | Why It Matters |
|---|---|
| Normal Fluid Service | Common process piping category |
| Category D Fluid Service | Limited-hazard service meeting specific code conditions |
| Category M Fluid Service | Particularly hazardous toxic fluid service |
| High Pressure Fluid Service | Special requirements under Chapter IX |
| High Purity Fluid Service | Special requirements under Chapter X |

A QC inspector should not classify piping solely by the fluid name or operating pressure. The owner and engineering documentation establish the applicable classification under the code.

## Lesson 1C — Inspection versus examination

This distinction is fundamental for refinery QC.

**Examination** involves quality-control activities such as visual examination, radiography, ultrasonic examination, and other required methods performed by qualified examination personnel.

**Inspection** concerns verification of compliance by the inspector identified under the governing code and project arrangements.

These responsibilities can interact, but they are not interchangeable.

A radiographer performing RT is not automatically the person authorized to approve the entire piping system for code compliance.

## Practice Question 1

**Scenario:** A contractor completes a 12-inch carbon-steel process piping weld at a refinery. The weld is visually examined and RT is requested. The superintendent tells you the weld is acceptable because the RT technician reported no rejectable indications.

**What should the QC Manager do?**

- A. Release the weld because RT passed.
- B. Verify the governing code, required examinations, documented results, qualifications, and project requirements before release. ✓
- C. Require every weld to receive 100% RT regardless of service.
- D. Allow production to determine code acceptance.

**Answer: B** — An RT pass alone does not constitute acceptance. The QC Manager must verify: the governing code (B31.3), the applicable fluid service classification, required examination type and extent per the engineering design, that the RT technician is qualified, that the WPS/PQR are qualified per Section IX, that the welder is qualified, that VT was performed and documented, and that all project-specific requirements are met. Only then can the authorized inspector release the weld.
